Election setup console

Phase A: what is authored before the poll opens. This origin has no part in the poll itself — the service runs with it stopped.

Absent · A7 What survives a restart The two key pairs, the electoral register and the wallet personas are written to disk here and reloaded. The ballot box, the hash chain and the released-token register are in memory and are lost. A real ballot box does not forget its contents when its process stops; this one does, and says so. At the close it writes the configuration, the ledger and the release register into closed/ beside the keys — the published record of a finished poll, which is not the same as a registry that survives.
Substituted · S3 The electoral register is a handful of invented people Enrolment is outside the system by construction: the register derives from civil registration under the law of the jurisdiction, and the office administers it without populating it. Here you type identifiers into a box. Presence in the register is the whole of the eligibility statement — there is no second predicate — which is why this console can hand somebody a vote and why a deployment would not have this page.
Absent · A5 Selections are stored in clear The schedule and the tally interval set below are recorded rather than enforced, and the ballot box holds each selection as it was cast. Where no running tally is published the design says the selection should be encrypted under a key held in shares by several independent bodies, so that an operator cannot read a ballot before the close. This build does not do that.
There is no election on this deployment yet Nothing has been authored here: no keys, no register, no configuration. Every other origin refuses with 503 until this console mints the first election, and the service will not invent one on its own — an election that appears because a process started is not an election.

Publication cadence

How often the box publishes the figures, in seconds. The close is always a publication point, so an interval longer than the poll means the close is the only one — which is what a small electorate wants, since what matters is how many voters stand behind each published number. Fixed once the ballot box is open: an interval is a promise about a running poll, so it has to be made before the poll runs.

Electoral register

Presence in the register is the whole of the eligibility statement. The office administers it and never populates it — here, enrolment also mints a wallet persona, which is the demo standing in for a citizen who already holds a qualified certificate.

    Which election is served

    Every election this deployment has minted is still on disk with its keys, its register and — if its poll was closed rather than interrupted — its record. That is what lets a ledger somebody downloaded months ago still be checked against the configuration it was published under. Serving one again is read-only: the box comes back shut, nothing resumes and no chain continues.

    This chooses among what is already there. It is not a way to put an election on the disk — minting is, and it is the section below.

    Switching voids every page anybody has open. A different election means different keys, a different election.json and a different digest, so every surface watching the configuration will say it is stale and ask to be reloaded. It is right to: what those pages pinned no longer describes what the origins serve. The pointer moves with it, so a later restart serves the same election.

    Absent · A7 A poll that was interrupted does not come back The election survives a restart and its keys and register survive; the ballot box, the hash chain and the released-token register do not. An election below whose poll shows no record was interrupted rather than closed, and no amount of serving it again recovers what it held. Its released-token count is unknown rather than nought, and the table says so.

    Mint a new election

    This is not an amendment Every key pair is replaced: the office's token key, the office's statement key, the box's statement key. Every token already issued stops verifying, every receipt stops being evidence, and the fingerprint a voter pinned stops being the fingerprint of the election they pinned it for. The ballot box and the released-token register are discarded with them. That is what a different election is — which is why this origin is one the poll runs without.

    One shape for both kinds. A referendum is a question with its answers as the options; an election is who should the representative be with the candidates as the options. Leave both empty and the ballot is numbered, as every election here was until now.

    The wording goes into election.json, so it is covered by the digest every client pins and every surface watches. That is the reason to put it there rather than anywhere else, and it is also the cost: changing a name after voters have pinned the configuration is the same event as changing anything else in it. The ballot still carries an integer, and nothing the box accepts or counts depends on a name.

    Generate an election

    Substituted · S8 Nobody will have chosen these votes The server fills the election itself, and every signature in it is genuine: each citizen gets an ad-hoc key pair, blinds it, has the stand-in wallet sign the request, receives the office's blind signature through the same checks as anybody else, unblinds it and signs a ballot. Nothing downstream can tell, which is the whole reason for generating one — and exactly why the label cannot be left to the data to imply. What is substituted is the electorate and their preferences. Every surface will say so, and there is no way to turn that off.
    One act, and the poll is closed at the end of it The election is minted, filled and closed together. A generated election that could still be voted in would be an election nobody could describe afterwards, so nothing can be added to it once this finishes. While it runs, every origin but this console refuses: a half-filled poll is a poll nobody should see, least of all a voter who could add a ballot to it. Reckon on about a minute per thousand ballots at 3072 bits.
