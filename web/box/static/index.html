Electronic Ballot Box — operator

The registry as the box holds it, and the one act that closes the poll.

What does not survive a restart The registry, the hash chain and the accepted ballots are in memory. A real ballot box does not forget its contents when its process stops, and the receipts voters hold would then refer to a chain that no longer exists — which is exactly the evidence the design says they are. Each entry stores the hash of the one before it, so the head depends on every record and on their order. A voter holding a receipt can show a discrepancy rather than merely allege one.

Closing the poll

An act of the box performed by an operator, not a clock (E1). Until it happens the records are withheld and only commitments are published; afterwards every record is released in clear, accepted and rejected alike, and anyone can recount. The box is also the sole witness to its own stopping — which is what supervision by competing parties would answer, and this demo cannot.

Tally

Not computable before the close: with no running tally configured, there is no result to be had — for anyone, the operator included. Out-of-range selections are reported by the distinct value each voter cast rather than merged into one figure, so an organised protest is not flattened into "invalid".
